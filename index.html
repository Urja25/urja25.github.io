01About

Where the pipette meets the pipeline

I work on both sides of the bench. In the lab, I developed and optimised single-cell DNA (HIPSD-seq) and 10X Multiome protocols for peripheral blood. At the terminal, I build the end-to-end pipelines, in Python, R and Nextflow on HPC, that turn that data into variant calls, clonal phylogenies and tests for selection. Carrying a single-cell cancer-genomics project the whole way, from assay development to computational analysis, is the combination I bring.

My PhD asked one question in Li-Fraumeni syndrome: does chromosomal instability build up in the blood of germline TP53 variant carriers before any cancer appears, and can positive selection be detected that early?

02Research

What I work on

Chromosomal instability & cancer predisposition

When and whether genome instability arises in the blood of people carrying pathogenic germline TP53 variants, years ahead of overt malignancy, and what tolerating that instability requires.

Variant calling & tool benchmarking

Somatic SNV and copy-number calling, mutational signature analysis, and systematic benchmarking of bioinformatics tools across single-cell and bulk genomics pipelines, so a call can be trusted before anything is built on it.

Clonal evolution & selection

Reconstructing clonal phylogenies and distinguishing neutral drift from positive selection, using MEDICC2 phylogenetic inference and SCIFER clonal-selection modelling across single-cell and bulk data.

Beyond the thesis

As a side project, I called copy-number from cell-free DNA (cfDNA) for a bladder cancer study. Before my PhD I built translational and machine-learning work: an alert system for early diagnosis of acute kidney injury, and in-silico biomarker analysis distinguishing glioma stem cells from normal neural stem cells.
